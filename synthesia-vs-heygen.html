The detail

What each one is genuinely better at

Synthesia: the safe corporate choice

What works

  • Largest stock avatar library, with consistent brand kits.
  • Document-to-video turns a policy PDF into a training module.
  • 140+ languages with a single presenter identity.

What doesn't

  • Output can feel institutional — fine for compliance, weak for social.
  • Editing is template-led rather than timeline-led.
  • The cheap plan caps minutes quickly.

HeyGen: the creator's translator

What works

  • Lip-synced translation is the best in this price band.
  • Custom avatars make a personal brand scale.
  • Fast rendering turns around social video in minutes.

What doesn't

  • Enterprise permissions and approvals are thinner.
  • Credit costs are easy to burn through.
  • Fewer stock presenters than Synthesia.

Verdict

The recommendation

Buy Synthesia for governance, HeyGen for reach

If a compliance or onboarding video has to be approved, translated and reused for a year, Synthesia's library and document workflow win. If you are one person producing multilingual video for an audience, HeyGen's custom avatar and translation justify the extra $10 a month. Neither is worth it for editing existing footage.

FAQ

Two questions buyers always ask

Can I use these avatars commercially?

On paid plans, generally yes — but the terms differ by avatar type and by whether the avatar is a stock presenter or your own likeness. Check the licence on the plan you buy before putting a video in a paid ad.

Do I need a studio or a camera?

No for stock avatars: text in, video out. A custom avatar needs a short recording session on your phone or webcam. That recording is the only “shoot” in the process.
