Synthesia vs InVideo: a presenter, or a stock-footage editor?

Both turn a script into a finished video in minutes, and they are not substitutes. One puts a consistent human on screen and expects you to have something to explain. The other assembles stock footage, voiceover and captions around a prompt. The $19-versus-$20 entry price is a coincidence, not a comparison.

The detail

Where each one earns the subscription

Synthesia: the compliance-safe presenter

What works

  • The largest stock avatar library, with brand kits that keep a series consistent.
  • Document-to-video turns a policy PDF into a module without a shoot.
  • One presenter identity across 140+ languages, which is hard to replicate elsewhere.

What doesn't

  • The output can feel institutional — correct for compliance, weak for social.
  • Editing is template-led rather than timeline-led.
  • The cheap plan caps minutes quickly, and extra minutes are where the bill grows.

InVideo: volume without a camera

What works

  • A genuine first draft from one prompt, with stock, voiceover and captions already in place.
  • Frontier model access inside the same editor, so quality can be raised per scene.
  • Timeline editing means the result is not locked to the prompt.

What doesn't

  • Credit costs per model are hard to predict, which makes budgeting awkward.
  • Every export needs a manual pass before it is publishable.
  • Program terms are reported rather than published — confirm before you build around it.

Verdict

The recommendation

Different jobs, so the decision is the deliverable

If the video has to teach someone how to do something and stay accurate for a year, Synthesia is the buy: consistent presenters, document input and translations that do not need a re-shoot. If the video has to exist by Friday and nobody needs to appear in it, InVideo is the buy: a usable draft from a prompt, then a timeline pass. Choosing on price alone gets this wrong, because the two produce different kinds of video.

FAQ

Three questions buyers ask

Can Synthesia make social clips?

It can export vertical video and the avatars hold up, but the templates and pacing are built for training. For TikTok-style volume you are better served by InVideo or OpusClip, which are designed around stock footage and short cuts rather than a presenter explaining something.

Which is cheaper for ten videos a month?

Depends on length and credits, not the headline price. Synthesia bills by video minutes and its cheap plan caps them quickly; InVideo's credit cost varies by the model you pick. Price the plan you would need for ten real videos, then compare — the entry plans rarely survive that test.

Will either one edit footage I already shot?

No. Both are generation tools: they produce video from a script or a prompt. If you already have footage and need cuts, captions or audio repair, use a timeline editor such as Descript or VEED instead.
