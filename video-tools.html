FAQ

Video tools, answered

Which is better, Synthesia or HeyGen?

For internal training and corporate comms, Synthesia: the avatars are more consistent and the localisation workflow is built for L&D. For creator content and multilingual publishing, HeyGen: translation with lip-sync is noticeably better, and custom avatars are easier to make. Both start around $19–$29 a month.

Are AI avatars allowed in ads?

It depends on the platform and the jurisdiction, not the tool. Meta and Google require disclosure of realistic synthetic media in some ad formats, and the EU AI Act imposes transparency duties. Synthesia and HeyGen both provide disclosure guidance — read it before you publish, and never present an avatar as a real spokesperson without consent.

Can I clone my own voice and likeness?

Yes on both platforms, with consent verification. You record a short sample, confirm you own the rights, and the platform creates a private avatar or voice. Cloning someone else without written permission will get your account banned and may be illegal.

Which video tool has the best affiliate program?

Synthesia: 25% recurring for 12 months with a 90-day cookie. HeyGen pays 20% for 12 months with a 30-day cookie, or 35% for three months on its creator track. VEED pays 30% for 12 months. All three are free to join.

Do I need editing skills?

Less than you think. InVideo and Opus Clip produce a draft you can publish with light edits. VEED and Descript assume you want editorial control. Synthesia and HeyGen remove editing entirely — you write a script and the tool assembles the video.
